The first release: Jak and Daxter: The Precursor Legacy, Jak II and Jak 3 in VR, on Quest and on PC VR.

Set up
- Each game is made once from your own disc image (.iso): no game files come with this release. Every disc version works.
- Each game keeps its own VR options; the launcher can set all three at once.

Playing
- First person through Jak's eyes, or the game's own camera around you; switch any time in the VR menu.
- Your hands are Jak's: his arms follow yours, his fingers follow your grip, and his hands stop at walls and floors.
- Body shown: see his whole body, or only some of it (body and arms, body and legs, arms and legs, arms only, legs
  only, hands only). His hands always stay.
- Physical melee: punch, spin kick and uppercut with your own fists, while on foot.
- Jak II and 3: grip at your hip or over your shoulder to draw the gun, aim it with your hand, steady it with your left
  hand on its front. Back there and let go to put it away.
- Vibration from the game, and a buzz as a hand meets a wall or the floor.
- Smooth or snap turning, moving where you look or where your left hand points, physical crouch, seated play.
- Cutscenes and talks through his eyes, around you, or on a big screen.

Graphics
- Max draw distance: the whole level drawn at full detail as far as you can see, so nothing pops in.
- Eye resolution, refresh rate, anti-aliasing, anisotropic filtering, foveated rendering and smoother frames, with
  Performance, Balanced and Quality presets.

Recommended performance options (the Graphics tab's presets set them at once)
- Quest 2: Performance. 72 or 80 Hz, 80% eye resolution, high foveation, smoother frames and boost on, Max draw
  distance off (turn it on if the game stays smooth where you play).
- Quest 3 and 3S: Balanced (the defaults). The headset's own refresh rate, 100% eyes, medium foveation, smoother
  frames and boost on, Max draw distance on, 2x anti-aliasing.
- PC VR: Balanced, or Quality on a strong graphics card (120% eyes, 4x anti-aliasing, 16x anisotropic filtering).
  Smoother frames, foveation and boost are the Quest's; on PC your headset's software has its own.
- Stutters? Lower the eye resolution first, then turn Max draw distance off.

The launcher
- Graphics, VR Body and VR Controls tabs with every option of the in-game VR menu.
- Controls for every controller: Quest Touch, Index, Vive, Windows Mixed Reality, Pico and more.
- One button gathers the logs into a zip for a bug report.
- Updates from the project's GitHub releases.

Multiplayer (Jak 1)
- Play together: Multiplayer is built in and on, and changes nothing until you host or join a game (turn it off in the
  launcher's Mods; on the Quest, Jak 1's options page). The others' Jaks run in your world with their names over them,
  and what one takes, everyone has. Every VR feature stays on for everyone, whoever hosts.
- From the VR menu: Host a game (which save?), Join a game (a list with each host's level and power cells), Play which
  save? (the host's progress, one of yours, or a new game), Players (the host can take one out). Games on your network
  are found by themselves; over the internet, through a party server (party_server.py, in the PC release's
  party-server folder) or a friend's address. Everyone needs the same release. How: MULTIPLAYER.md, beside
  party_server.py and in the project's docs.
- Built on Teamruns' multiplayer.

Mods
- Texture packs for all three games, on PC and Quest: add a pack's zip (OpenGOAL's own kind), turn each on or off and
  put them in order; the game is built again with them from your disc.
- On the Quest, Precursor's mod packages as before. How: MODS.md in the project's docs.
